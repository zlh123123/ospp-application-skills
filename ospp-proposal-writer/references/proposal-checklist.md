# Proposal Checklist

## Official coverage

- The current project title and ID are correct.
- All current mandatory requirements are addressed.
- Optional work is visibly separated from required work.
- The schedule covers the official duration.
- The requested output format and filename convention are satisfied.

## Repository grounding

- Existing behavior and proposed behavior are not mixed.
- Named code locations exist at the recorded branch or revision.
- Compatibility boundaries are described where the project explicitly requires them.
- Build, test, and runtime claims match actual evidence.
- External references have a concrete reuse role.

## Feasibility

- Each promised feature has a verification method.
- Tests, benchmark, dataset, model, infrastructure, and hardware assumptions are visible.
- The plan includes integration, documentation, reporting, and upstream review.
- The scope does not depend on several unresolved high-risk components succeeding simultaneously.
- Mentor questions are kept outside the application unless their uncertainty belongs in the plan.

## Applicant evidence

- Experience claims are supported by the resume or public artifacts.
- No local-only work is described as an upstream contribution.
- The application does not repeat the resume without connecting experience to project feasibility.

## Writing and artifact quality

- The document is direct and project-specific, without ceremonial or promotional filler.
- Paragraphs carry reasoning; bullets and tables are used only where they improve comparison or scheduling.
- Links resolve and diagrams add information rather than decoration.
- There are no TODO markers, unsupported targets, conflicting dates, or unexplained acronyms.
- If delivered as PDF, every page has been visually checked for clipping, tiny diagrams, broken links, and malformed tables.
